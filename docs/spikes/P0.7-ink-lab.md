# Spike P0.7 — Ink lab

**Question:** can handwriting in the browser feel good enough, especially on iPad + Apple Pencil, to build our own ink engine (decision 0004)?

**Status:** Prototype built and tested in headless Chromium. **Waiting for real-device results.** Only someone holding a pen can answer the question.

## What the prototype does
Open **NoteDoco → Ink lab** (`/NoteDoco/#/lab/ink` once deployed).

- Pens: ballpoint, fountain, marker, highlighter (always behind ink), eraser. Five ink colours, three sizes, lined/grid/dot/blank paper, undo/redo, clear.
- Pen input at full rate (coalesced events) with prediction ahead of the pen tip.
- Palm rejection: the pen always wins; touches are ignored while the pen is down or hovering and for 300 ms after; large contacts count as palms; a touch stroke that began just before the pen landed is erased.
- Fingers draw only until a pen is seen, then they pan and pinch-zoom. Two-finger tap = undo, three-finger tap = redo. The pen's eraser end or barrel button erases.
- A **Measurements** panel and **Copy report**, which puts a JSON report on the clipboard.
- **Pen settings** switches each technique on and off, so you can compare them: full-rate samples, prediction, low-latency canvas, the Chrome/Edge system ink trail and raw updates, pressure, finger drawing, palm size.

The engine is `src/lab/ink/engine.ts`. The parts Phase 3 will reuse are in `src/canvas/`: the palm-rejection router, point encoding and stroke styles.

## How to test (about 10 minutes per device)
1. On the iPad, open the preview in Safari, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**, and open it from the Home Screen. That's how people will use it, and it behaves differently from a Safari tab.
2. Open **Ink lab**. Write a few lines of normal handwriting with your hand resting on the screen as you would on paper. Draw some circles and fast scribbles.
3. Note anything that feels off: lag behind the pen tip, jagged curves, marks from your palm, strokes that break, or line weight that doesn't follow pressure.
4. Tap **Measurements → Copy report** and paste the report into the chat.
5. Optional: in **Pen settings**, turn off *Predict ahead of the pen*, write again, and say which feels better. Then do the same for *Low-latency canvas*.
6. If you have a Windows or Android pen device, repeat there, and try the pen's eraser end or button.

## What we're looking for (go/no-go)
| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Pen samples / s (iPad, full-rate on) | ≥ 200 (Apple Pencil reports 240) |
| Event → frame, p95 | < 1 frame (16 ms at 60 Hz; 8 ms on 120 Hz ProMotion iPads) |
| Paint cost, p95 | < 4 ms |
| Palm marks over a page of writing | None |
| Feel | "Close to a native notes app" |

## Headless baseline (not representative)
Automated tests (`e2e/ink-lab.spec.ts`) send real pen events with pressure and tilt through the Chrome DevTools protocol. Strokes render with pressure-varying width, the eraser removes strokes, undo/redo work, and the report is produced with `coalescedEvents: true` and `predictedEvents: true`. Paint cost was 0.3 ms per frame with 5 bytes stored per point. Synthetic events arrive one per frame, so sample rate and latency numbers from headless runs mean nothing; only device runs count.
